Whole Home Renovation in Everett, WA

Coordinate a whole-home remodel in Everett and Snohomish County: room priorities, systems, layout, permits, phasing and a written construction scope.

One coordinated scope for a home that needs more than one room

NW General Contractor reviews whole-home renovation projects in Everett and Snohomish County. The starting point is what you want the home to do better and what needs repair. We help define the work across rooms and building systems before treating finishes as a shopping list.

Work to assess together

  • Layout and circulation: walls, doorways, storage and relationships between rooms.
  • Kitchens and bathrooms: dimensions, fixtures, cabinets, surfaces and utility connections.
  • Building systems: electrical, plumbing and heating work coordinated with the relevant trades.
  • Envelope and condition: windows, siding, insulation, moisture and repair needs.
  • Finish continuity: floors, trim, paint and transitions between retained and new work.

A room-by-room plan with shared costs visible

List what stays, what changes and what remains unknown in every room. Keep building-wide work separate so it is neither omitted nor counted several times. The whole-home budget guide helps prepare that schedule.

Phasing, occupancy and access

Decide which spaces must remain usable and which stages need separation or temporary relocation. Discuss protection, storage, safe access and utility interruptions. Phasing can reduce a single period of disruption but may repeat setup and extend the overall schedule. Agree on those tradeoffs before work starts.

Investigate problems before covering them

Existing leaks, uneven floors or deteriorated support members can affect both design and budget. Review foundation and drainage assessment questions and bring existing reports. A finish renovation alone does not diagnose or correct concealed structural or moisture problems.

Before demolition in older homes, identify materials that need assessment and the appropriate qualified trades. For pre-1978 housing, review EPA lead-safe renovation guidance before disturbing painted surfaces. Do not treat do-it-yourself demolition as an automatic saving; protection, testing, disposal and repair of unintended damage belong in the decision.

Written scope, changes and closeout

  • Define dimensions and quantities: room or wall area, cabinet runs, fixtures and the parts of the home being changed.
  • Separate retained items from replacements and identify who purchases each product.
  • Identify design, engineering, permits, protection, demolition, labor, materials, disposal, finishing and sales tax. Mark exclusions explicitly.
  • List allowances with their quantities and installed-versus-supply-only basis. Record how selection changes affect the total.
  • Keep an owner contingency separate from the contract price. Agree in writing how concealed conditions and owner changes are authorized.

Ask for milestone communication, inspection responsibilities, completion criteria and closeout documents. We do not guarantee a construction duration, percentage energy saving or resale return before the actual project has been assessed.

Local permit planning

Start with the parcel jurisdiction. An Everett mailing address does not establish that the property is inside city limits. Use Everett Permit Services for city property and county residential permit guidance for unincorporated Snohomish County. Ask which building, plumbing, mechanical, electrical or site-work approvals apply to the actual scope. A building-permit exemption does not waive other applicable requirements.

Prepare your renovation discussion

Share the address, room priorities, existing plans, photos and target budget. Include whether an addition or a focused kitchen remodel is also under consideration. NW Style Homes 1 LLC operates NW General Contractor under Washington registration NWSTYSH768DA; review our contractor disclosure.

Questions before you start

Should I renovate everything at once?

Compare a coordinated phase with staged work using the same scope. Occupancy, access, financing and repeated setup may change which approach fits.

Can I get a price per square foot?

A rate without scope can hide major differences. Ask for room and system costs, inclusions, exclusions and allowances before comparing totals.
