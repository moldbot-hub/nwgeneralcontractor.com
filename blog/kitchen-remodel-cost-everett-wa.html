Kitchen Remodel Cost in Everett: Build a Comparable Budget

Compare Everett kitchen remodel estimates by scope, allowances, installation, tax and contingency. Includes a clearly illustrative budget example.

By NW General Contractor. Updated October 11, 2026 after a substantive scope and source review.

What will your kitchen remodel cost?

A useful price needs a measured scope. Cabinet refacing in an existing layout is different from new cabinetry, a relocated sink or a structural opening. We do not have a verified local project-price dataset to publish as an Everett average. Use this guide to build an estimate you can compare, then request a property-specific price from our kitchen remodeling team.

Choose the scope before the finishes

  • Refresh: retain sound cabinet boxes and the layout; price the exact refinishing, counters, hardware and surface work.
  • Replace in the existing layout: identify cabinet dimensions, appliances, flooring transitions, electrical and plumbing connections.
  • Reconfigure: add design and any structural assessment, wall changes, utility relocation and related permit work.

An example that adds up

Illustration only: $40,000 before sales tax and owner contingency. These invented planning inputs demonstrate arithmetic; they are not a quote, completed project, typical Everett price or promise that this scope can be built for this amount. Replace every line with actual proposal figures.

Illustrative line Amount
Cabinet supply and hardware $10,000
Countertop supply and fabrication $5,000
Appliances supplied by owner $4,000
Flooring, tile and paint materials $3,000
Installation and trade labor $12,000
Protection, demolition and disposal $2,000
Design and permit allowance $2,000
Management and other stated project costs $2,000
Total before tax and owner contingency $40,000

For this arithmetic example, a separately held $4,000 owner contingency would make the pre-tax planning envelope $44,000. That contingency is not a recommended universal percentage or money owed to the contractor. Confirm the applicable tax and avoid counting labor twice when a supplier gives an installed price.

Make competing proposals comparable

  • Define dimensions and quantities: room or wall area, cabinet runs, fixtures and the parts of the home being changed.
  • Separate retained items from replacements and identify who purchases each product.
  • Identify design, engineering, permits, protection, demolition, labor, materials, disposal, finishing and sales tax. Mark exclusions explicitly.
  • List allowances with their quantities and installed-versus-supply-only basis. Record how selection changes affect the total.
  • Keep an owner contingency separate from the contract price. Agree in writing how concealed conditions and owner changes are authorized.

Why two kitchens with the same area can differ

Cabinet run length, storage hardware, countertop seams, access, protection of occupied rooms and the condition behind finishes all matter. A wall opening can require assessment of the load path; moving a fixture can involve more than its visible connection. Ask which assumptions were made without opening walls and how those assumptions will be checked.

Before demolition in older homes, identify materials that need assessment and the appropriate qualified trades. For pre-1978 housing, review EPA lead-safe renovation guidance before disturbing painted surfaces. Do not treat do-it-yourself demolition as an automatic saving; protection, testing, disposal and repair of unintended damage belong in the decision.

Permits and time out of the kitchen

Start with the parcel jurisdiction. An Everett mailing address does not establish that the property is inside city limits. Use Everett Permit Services for city property and county residential permit guidance for unincorporated Snohomish County. Ask which building, plumbing, mechanical, electrical or site-work approvals apply to the actual scope. A building-permit exemption does not waive other applicable requirements.

Distinguish selection and ordering, permit review, site preparation, construction, inspections and closeout. Request a project schedule tied to those dependencies rather than a universal completion window. The kitchen timeline guide explains what to ask.

Should resale value decide the budget?

Choose work for the function and condition you need, then ask a local real-estate professional about comparable properties if resale is a priority. A national or regional cost-recovery percentage is not a forecast for your home. Do not assume renovation spending increases market value dollar for dollar.

Questions before you start

Can you price the kitchen from photos?

Photos help identify questions, but dimensions, selections, access and concealed conditions can affect the price. Ask what is included in an initial estimate and what needs a visit.

How do I reduce the scope?

Separate required repairs from optional finishes. Compare retaining a workable layout and serviceable products with replacing them; ask for the difference in the written proposal.
