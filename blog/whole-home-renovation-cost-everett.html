Whole Home Renovation Cost in Everett: Build a Room-by-Room Scope

Plan a whole-home renovation budget with room priorities, building systems, occupancy, allowances and change control in Everett and Snohomish County.

By NW General Contractor. Updated October 11, 2026 after a substantive scope and source review.

A useful whole-home budget starts with boundaries

Two homes of the same size can need very different work. A finish update, a layout change and replacement of aging systems should not share one unexplained square-foot allowance. We do not publish a universal Everett rate without verified scope and price data. Use a room-by-room schedule to request a whole-home renovation estimate.

Make a scope register

  • For each room, list what stays, what is repaired and what is replaced.
  • List building-wide systems separately so electrical, plumbing, heating and envelope work is not counted in several rooms.
  • Identify proposed wall changes and structural questions before choosing finishes.
  • Record owner-supplied products, installation responsibility and selection deadlines.
  • Mark essential work and optional phases.

Include the work between the rooms

  • Define dimensions and quantities: room or wall area, cabinet runs, fixtures and the parts of the home being changed.
  • Separate retained items from replacements and identify who purchases each product.
  • Identify design, engineering, permits, protection, demolition, labor, materials, disposal, finishing and sales tax. Mark exclusions explicitly.
  • List allowances with their quantities and installed-versus-supply-only basis. Record how selection changes affect the total.
  • Keep an owner contingency separate from the contract price. Agree in writing how concealed conditions and owner changes are authorized.

Hallway transitions, trim matching, dust containment, temporary protection and final touchups are easy to miss when individual rooms are priced separately. Ask whether utility interruptions, storage, furniture moving and temporary accommodation are included or owner responsibilities.

Sequence occupied and vacant projects differently

A house can be physically accessible yet unsuitable for normal occupancy during a stage of work. Discuss safe routes, usable bathrooms, cooking facilities, temporary utilities and separation from construction. A phased project may duplicate setup and extend disruption; a single phase may require temporary relocation. Compare the complete arrangements.

Investigate before relying on a fixed scope

Before demolition in older homes, identify materials that need assessment and the appropriate qualified trades. For pre-1978 housing, review EPA lead-safe renovation guidance before disturbing painted surfaces. Do not treat do-it-yourself demolition as an automatic saving; protection, testing, disposal and repair of unintended damage belong in the decision.

Known water or structural concerns should inform the plan before finishes hide them. Our foundation and drainage guide explains useful observations and assessment limits. Do not assume new finishes resolve the underlying condition.

Budget changes transparently

A good comparison shows fixed work, allowances, exclusions and unresolved conditions separately. Keep a decision log for selections and written approval of changed work. Ask for the price and schedule effect before approving a change. A contingency is planning capacity, not evidence that a particular hidden problem exists.

Permits and next steps

Start with the parcel jurisdiction. An Everett mailing address does not establish that the property is inside city limits. Use Everett Permit Services for city property and county residential permit guidance for unincorporated Snohomish County. Ask which building, plumbing, mechanical, electrical or site-work approvals apply to the actual scope. A building-permit exemption does not waive other applicable requirements.

Bring your room register, photos, existing reports and budget boundary. Use our kitchen budget example for that room and the addition guide if expansion is being considered.
