Siding Cost in Everett, WA (2026 Guide)

Published July 2026 by NW General Contractor

For 2026, siding cost Everett homeowners can use for early planning typically ranges from about $7–$18 per square foot installed, while many complete replacements may fall around $18,000–$35,000. Your actual price depends on measured wall area, material, labor, access, finishes, tear-off, and repairs discovered beneath the old siding.

What a siding costs in Everett

There is no single average cost of a siding in Everett that applies to every house. Contractors price exterior wall area, not interior living space, and two homes with similar floor plans can have very different siding quantities. Multiple stories, gables, bump-outs, windows, doors, corners, and attached structures all change the amount of cutting, flashing, trim, staging, and labor required.

For preliminary budgeting, published 2026 Everett estimates suggest installed siding prices typically fall within about $7–$11 per square foot for vinyl, $10–$14 per square foot for engineered wood, and $12–$18 per square foot for fiber cement, depending on product line and scope. Local full-replacement planning ranges commonly extend to approximately $18,000–$35,000 for a house with a typical residential layout. These are planning figures, not a substitute for an on-site estimate. [Everett siding cost benchmark](https://www.redravenexteriors.com/siding/everett)

A siding price per square foot in Everett should be compared only after confirming what it includes. One contractor may include removal, disposal, weather-resistive barrier, flashing, trim, caulking, paint, and cleanup, while another may quote siding installation alone. Ask whether sales tax, permits, equipment, delivery, and allowances for sheathing repairs are included.

  • Vinyl siding: typically about $7–$11 per square foot installed, depending on scope and product.
  • Engineered wood siding: typically about $10–$14 per square foot installed, depending on finish and detailing.
  • Fiber-cement siding: typically about $12–$18 per square foot installed, depending on finish and access.
  • Complete re-siding: often planned around $18,000–$35,000 for a typical house, with larger or more complex projects potentially outside that range.

What drives the price

Project scope is usually the strongest cost driver. Installing siding over a prepared wall is different from removing several layers, replacing damaged sheathing, correcting flashing, or rebuilding trim around openings. Moisture-related damage cannot always be quantified until removal begins, so a responsible proposal should explain how concealed repairs will be documented and priced.

Materials affect both purchase price and labor. Vinyl panels are comparatively light and quick to handle, while fiber-cement products require different cutting, fastening, clearance, and handling procedures. Engineered wood, cedar, metal, and specialty panels each have their own installation details. Product thickness, profile, exposure, factory color, matching accessories, and manufacturer-required components can change the total.

Finishes matter as well. Primed siding that will be painted on site requires surface preparation, paint, labor, and suitable weather. Factory-finished products may have a higher material price but reduce field painting. Trim width, accent walls, soffits, fascia, corner boards, window surrounds, and transitions between materials should be specified instead of buried in a general finish allowance.

Labor rises when crews need scaffolding, lift access, extensive ladder work, or additional protection for neighboring property and landscaping. Everett homes on tight lots, sloped sites, or busy streets may require more setup and material handling. Permits, inspections, disposal, delivery, and temporary weather protection can also affect the final price.

  • Measured wall area, stories, gables, corners, windows, doors, and architectural details.
  • Removal and disposal of existing siding, including the number and type of existing layers.
  • Condition of sheathing, framing, flashing, weather barrier, and trim after tear-off.
  • Material profile, finish system, accessory package, and installation requirements.
  • Crew access, staging, lifts, landscaping protection, cleanup, permits, and inspections.

Cost ranges by project scope

A targeted repair in the Everett area may typically run about $800–$3,000 when damage is limited and compatible replacement material is available. A larger section or one-wall replacement may commonly fall around $3,000–$8,000. Material-matching difficulties, work above the first story, widespread water damage, or custom trim can move either range higher.

A complete replacement is more likely to fall within approximately $18,000–$35,000 for a typical residential project, depending on measured siding area and material. Larger homes, complicated elevations, premium finishes, extensive sheathing work, or coordinated window and exterior-trim changes may exceed that planning range. A low proposal may also omit items included in a higher proposal, so compare scope before comparing totals.

The installation portion of a full re-side usually takes about 1–3 weeks for an average-size home, depending on crew size, material, architectural complexity, weather, inspections, and concealed repairs. The overall timeline may be longer after including product selection, ordering, scheduling, permit review when applicable, painting, and change-order work. How to get an accurate estimate

Start with an exterior inspection and field measurements. The estimator should identify siding type, wall dimensions, stories, openings, trim, penetrations, access limitations, and visible signs of moisture trouble. Share any known leaks, past repairs, pest activity, interior staining, or problem walls. Photographs can help with initial screening, but they rarely provide enough information for a dependable final price.

Request a written scope that separates demolition, disposal, wall preparation, siding, trim, flashing, weather barrier, fasteners, sealants, finishes, equipment, cleanup, and applicable permits. If concealed damage cannot be priced in advance, ask for labor and material ranges or a clearly defined change-order method. Confirm how discoveries will be photographed and approved before extra work proceeds.

Do not compare proposals based only on how much does a siding cost at the bottom of the page. Compare measured quantities, product names, profiles, finish specifications, excluded work, allowances, payment schedule, and cleanup responsibilities. Verify that each contractor is properly registered for the work and that the proposal addresses manufacturer installation requirements.

Permit needs depend on the property and work being performed. The City of Everett states that permits are required for most work unless an exemption applies, and its application materials classify exterior alteration as a project type. Confirm the requirement with Everett Permit Services before construction, particularly when the project changes materials, exposes or replaces sheathing, alters structural components, or affects a regulated property. Permit fees vary with project type, size, and valuation. [City of Everett Permit Services](https://www.everettwa.gov/169/Permit-Services)

Smart ways to save without cutting corners

Keep the exterior design focused. Using one primary siding material and a limited accent area generally reduces transitions, specialty trim, ordering complexity, and labor. Standard profiles and readily available colors can also be easier to source than custom products. Ask for alternatives that preserve the needed wall assembly while simplifying purely decorative details.

Repair can be economical when damage is truly isolated and compatible material is obtainable. Replacement may be more practical when failures appear across several walls, the existing product cannot be matched, or repeated repairs would require overlapping setup and finish work. Ask the contractor to price a defined repair option and a broader replacement option so you can compare scope, not assumptions.

Coordinate related exterior work before installation. Planned window, door, electrical, ventilation, or deck-ledger changes can affect siding and flashing. Completing those penetrations in the right sequence may avoid removing new material later. Preserve a contingency range for concealed sheathing or framing repairs instead of spending the entire budget on premium colors or accents.

Savings should not come from omitting flashing, weather management, correct clearances, compatible fasteners, substrate repairs, or manufacturer-required installation details. Those components are less visible than the finish but central to the assembly. A clear proposal and documented preparation provide more value than an unusually low number with major scope gaps.

  • Simplify material transitions, accent areas, and custom trim where they add little practical value.
  • Compare factory-finished and field-painted options using complete labor-and-material ranges.
  • Schedule related window, door, and exterior-penetration work in the correct sequence.
  • Keep a contingency range for concealed repairs discovered during tear-off.
  • Choose among complete, comparable scopes instead of selecting solely by the lowest total.
